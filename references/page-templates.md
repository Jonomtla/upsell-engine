# Upsell Page Templates

Impact Conversion Resources. Two skeletons cover nearly every upsell page. Which one you use depends on whether the offer is more of the same, or a different complementary product. Write every section; a template with skipped sections is a money grab with formatting.

## Choosing the format weight

From the frontend price (see offer-architecture Part G): under $50 the page is one section only (offer, discount, scarcity, urgency, no letter). $50 to $150 gets the full text template below. Over $150, write the template as a video script the owner can record, plus a short on-page fallback.

## Template A: more of the same

**Section 1: Authority open.** A credible figure congratulates them on the purchase. Real founder, real practitioner, real expert. Never invent credentials. Two to four sentences.

*Quality bar: reads like a person, names the specific product they just bought.*

**Section 2: Why this offer exists.** The reason a discount is appearing four seconds after they paid full price. A real reason: overstock, a first-order-only rule, a batch decision, founder's policy. Without this section the page reads as a cash grab; with it, a deal.

*Quality bar: the reason is concrete and believable, one short paragraph.*

**Section 3: The offer and its specifics.** Exactly how much more they get and what it costs against normal price. Numbers, not adjectives.

**Section 4: Benefits restated.** The same benefits that made them buy the frontend, now framed as "and you get even more of it". Use mined customer language here; their five-star reviews already wrote this section.

**Section 5: Price and CTA.** State the price plainly next to the button. Disclose any recurring charge at the same size as the price.

## Template B: complementary product

**Section 1: Reaffirm, then introduce.** Confirm the decision they just made was right, then present the new product as an addition. Never imply the first product wasn't enough; the offer is an addition, not a rescue.

**Section 2: Why they need it.** The next problem, named in the customer's own mined words. This section succeeds when the reader thinks "how did they know that's what happens".

**Section 3: Benefits plus mechanism.** What it does and how it works with the first product: faster results, longer-lasting results, or the next problem solved. Mechanism makes the claim believable; one clear mechanism beats three vague ones.

**Section 4: Offer and CTA.** Price, any discount with its reason, disclosure of anything recurring, button.

## Downsell pages

Much simpler: the offer and the discount, two or three sentences ("Are you sure? Here's the same result at half the commitment"), price, button. Do not write a letter for a downsell.

## The two always-rules (every page, both templates)

1. **"No thanks" stays clearly visible.** Hiding it makes people close the tab, and they never see the later offers.
2. **One line at the top: "Please read this whole page carefully."** Crude, effective, keep it.

## Subscription offers, extra requirements

Frame as keeping the result, not saving on refills. State the recurring price at full size next to the button, with billing frequency and how to cancel. A concealed recurring charge costs more brand damage than four disclosed offers ever will.

## Before shipping any page

Run the full checklist in the Copy Rules section of this system, then check: every claim traces to the product or a mined quote; every number is the owner's real number; the reason-this-exists section is present and believable; nothing implies the first purchase was insufficient.
