# Review Mining

Impact Conversion Resources. How to find the exact words customers use, and turn them into offers and copy. The core law: **the market has already written your copy. Your job is to find it.**

## How to search and harvest

1. **Use plain natural-language queries.** Search how a person types: "best descaler for espresso machines reddit", "[product] review didn't work", "[problem] tried everything". No search operators.
2. **Search to find the page, then fetch the page to lift exact words.** This is the rule most likely to be silently broken, so read it twice. Your search tool will hand you sentences formatted exactly like quotes ("one reviewer said 'they fell apart in a week'"). Those are paraphrases wearing quote marks. If you did not fetch the page the words sit on, the quote does not exist. Quote only from fetched page bodies; one rich page often yields ten or more quotes. When fetching, ask for exact verbatim sentences, and discard any quote that reads suspiciously polished. Keep a running list of every source that refused to fetch; it goes in the messaging file.
3. **Sources, in order of value:** their own product reviews, competitor product reviews on the same marketplaces, Amazon reviews of the category, Reddit and niche forums, YouTube comments on category videos. One-star reviews carry failure and skepticism language; five-star reviews carry desire and transformation language.

## The three message categories

Tag every quote as one of:

- **Motivation** (the before-story): desired outcomes, pain points, the trigger that started them looking
- **Value** (the why-us): benefits in their words, delighted surprises, the moment it proved itself, must-have requirements
- **Anxiety** (the what-if): uncertainties, objections, ways they've been burned before

## The mining passes

Run all six. The quote counts are what a rich consumer category yields; niche categories often can't feed them, so follow the shortfall protocol below rather than padding.

**The shortfall protocol.** Some platforms block fetching (Reddit and Amazon often do), and niche durable goods simply have fewer written words in the wild. When a pass stalls: try roughly three more search phrasings, then try the fallback sources (the store's own review widget, YouTube comments, retailer sites that stock the category, Trustpilot, specialist forums). If you're still short, present what you have and mark the shortfall explicitly: "Pass 3 yielded 4 quotes, not 8; here's what exists." A marked gap is a finding. Below about 4 real quotes on a pass, stop mining and ask the owner to paste reviews (their review platform export, or copy-pasted review pages); mine the pasted text under the same verbatim rules. A padded pass is a lie, and one invented quote destroys everything built on the file. Never invent, never quote from snippets to hit a number.

### Pass 1: Pain and desire (10+ quotes)
How they describe the problem and the wished-for outcome. Vivid, physical, specific beats generic. Searches: "[problem] what it's like", "[category] finally found one that works review".

### Pass 2: Their reviews plus competitor reviews (10+ quotes)
What buyers of this exact category praise and punish. Mine one-star for failure language, five-star for the exact benefit words that drove the purchase.

### Pass 3: The next problem (8+ quotes). **This pass matters most for upsells.**
What shows up after the product works. Searches: "now that I have [product] what else do I need", "[product] accessories worth it", "[category] beginner mistakes", "what nobody tells you about [product]". Every recurring "next problem" is a candidate post-purchase offer. Also mine questions: "how do I clean / maintain / store / get better results with [product]".

### Pass 4: Failed solutions (8+ quotes)
What they tried that didn't work, and the emotional residue. This feeds the "why this offer exists" sections and objection handling.

### Pass 5: Skepticism (6+ quotes)
The language of disbelief in this category: "gimmick", "worked for a week", "just buy the cheap one". Tells you which objections the upsell pages must kill and what proof they'll accept.

### Pass 6: Workarounds and rituals (6+ quotes)
The odd things customers do to cope or get more from the product. Hyper-specific "that's exactly me" material, and often a product idea hiding in plain sight.

## Output format, per quote

**SOURCE:** [platform and page]
**THE RAW:** "[exact quote from the fetched page]"
**THE COPY VERSION:** [the same words mirrored into a copy line]
**TAG:** [Motivation / Value / Anxiety] · [which pass]

## The Mirror Rule

The copy version stays as close to the raw quote as possible. Allowed changes: shift first person to second person; make the smallest tense adjustment that shift requires; break one long sentence into two. Nothing else.

Forbidden: adding words or ideas not in the raw, inventing metaphors, making it "better", building a quote from a search snippet. The test: every word in the copy version must trace back to a word in the raw. If you added something, delete it.

- Raw: "I didn't realise how much gunk was in my machine until I ran this through it" becomes "You won't realise how much gunk is in your machine until you run this through it."
- Wrong: adding "your morning ritual deserves better" when nobody said it.

## The messaging file

Compile everything organised by theme, not by source: Pain, Desire, Next Problems, Failed Solutions, Skepticism, Workarounds. Then add:

- **Top 10 power lines**, ranked, each tagged with its raw quote
- **The next-problem shortlist**: every recurring post-purchase problem, with quote count as evidence, ranked. This list goes straight into the Phase 4 problem map.
